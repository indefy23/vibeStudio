## When to use

Use this skill whenever you need to arrange clips, scenes, or elements in a specific order to tell a story, convey information, or create a desired emotional impact. Sequencing determines how content unfolds over time and is fundamental to video editing.

## Core Principles

### 1. What is Sequencing?
- **Definition**: The order and timing of visual and audio elements in a video.
- **Scope**: Can apply to entire video structure (acts, scenes) or small sections (shot order, text appearance).
- **Goal**: Create coherent narrative, maintain engagement, control pacing, and deliver message effectively.

### 2. Basic Sequence Structures
- **Linear/Chronological**: Events in time order (past → present → future). Most common.
- **Non-linear**: Jumps in time (flashbacks, flash-forwards).
- **Thematic**: Grouped by topic rather than time.
- **Problem-solution**: Present problem, then solution.
- **Compare-contrast**: Show two things side by side or sequentially.
- **Cause-effect**: Show cause, then effect.
- **Story arc**: Setup → conflict → resolution.
- **Three-act structure**: Beginning (setup), middle (confrontation), end (resolution).
- **Montage**: Series of short shots condensed to show passage of time or idea.

### 3. Shot Sequencing Principles
- **Establishing shot → Medium → Close-up**: Classic progression from wide to detail.
- **Shot-reverse-shot**: Conversation pattern (A → B → A).
- **Action-reaction**: Show action, then reaction (face, consequence).
- **Build-up**: Series of shots increasing in intensity or importance.
- **Payoff**: Setup something early, deliver later.
- **Foreshadowing**: Hint at future events.
- **Callbacks**: Reference earlier content for continuity or humor.

### 4. Pacing Through Sequencing
- **Fast sequence**: Many short shots (0.5-1s each). Creates energy, urgency, chaos.
- **Slow sequence**: Fewer, longer shots (3-10s). Creates contemplation, tension, importance.
- **Variable pacing**: Mix fast and slow for dynamic rhythm.
- **Accelerando**: Sequence gets progressively faster.
- **Ritardando**: Sequence gets progressively slower.
- **Cliffhanger**: End segment with unresolved tension to keep watching.
- **Beat**: Pause or moment of silence for impact.

### 5. Information Sequencing
- **Simple to complex**: Start with basics, build to advanced.
- **General to specific**: Broad overview, then details.
- **Chronological**: Past to present.
- **Priority**: Most important first (inverted pyramid).
- **Spatial**: Left to right, top to bottom, near to far.
- **Cause to effect**: Reason first, result second.
- **Problem to solution**: Issue first, fix second.
- **Question to answer**: Pose question, then answer.

### 6. Emotional Sequencing
- **Tension curve**: Build tension, release, build again.
- **Emotional journey**: Map emotions over time (happy → sad → hopeful).
- **Catharsis**: Build to emotional release.
- **Surprise**: Subvert expectations for impact.
- **Relief**: After tension, provide resolution.
- **Nostalgia**: Reference past to evoke sentiment.
- **Inspiration**: Build to motivational peak.

### 7. Multi-Layered Sequencing
- **A-roll/B-roll**: Main footage (A) intercut with supporting footage (B).
- **J-cut/L-cut**: Audio leads or lags video.
- **Parallel editing**: Two or more storylines intercut.
- **Cross-cutting**: Alternate between simultaneous actions.
- **Montage sequence**: Condensed time with many shots.
- **Split screen**: Show multiple sequences simultaneously.

### 8. Transition Sequencing
- **Hard cuts**: Most common. Use when content flows naturally.
- **Fades**: Indicate scene beginning/end or time passage.
- **Dissolves**: Show connection or time passage.
- **Wipes**: Indicate location change or section break.
- **Match cuts**: Create thematic connection.
- **Smash cuts**: Abrupt contrast for shock/humor.
- **No transition**: Direct cut. Most invisible.

### 9. Text & Graphic Sequencing
- **Lower thirds**: Appear when person introduced, stay until relevant.
- **Titles**: Show at beginning, stay for duration.
- **Callouts**: Pop in when point is made, disappear after.
- **Countdown timers**: Appear before event, count down.
- **Animated logos**: Intro/outro sequences.
- **Data visualization**: Build charts progressively.
- **Bullet points**: Reveal one by one as discussed.

### 10. Audio Sequencing
- **Music cues**: Change music with scene mood.
- **Sound bridges**: Audio spans transition.
- **Layering**: Add audio elements progressively.
- **Silence**: Use strategically for impact.
- **Voiceover pacing**: Match to visual pacing.
- **Sound effects timing**: Hit on visual events.
- **Ambience changes**: Match location.

## Technical Guidelines

### Timeline Organization
- **Tracks**: Organize by type (video, audio, titles, effects).
- **Clips**: Trim to essential content. No dead air.
- **Markers**: Use timeline markers for important beats or section starts.
- **Nested sequences**: Complex sections in sub-sequences.
- **Color coding**: Visual organization of clips/tracks.

### Timing Precision
- **Frame accuracy**: Edit to frame level (1/30s or 1/60s).
- **Beat alignment**: Sync cuts to musical beats.
- **Dialogue sync**: Ensure cuts don't interrupt speech.
- **Pause timing**: Use 0.5-2 second pauses for emphasis.
- **Reveal timing**: Text/graphics appear when needed, not before.

### Continuity
- **180° rule**: Maintain screen direction.
- **Eyeline matches**: Characters look in consistent directions.
- **Action continuity**: Actions flow logically across cuts.
- **Prop continuity**: Objects maintain consistent state.
- **Costume continuity**: Clothing matches across shots.
- **Lighting continuity**: Consistent lighting direction/quality.

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

- **Jump cuts without purpose**: Abrupt same-angle cuts feel jarring unless intentional.
- **Poor information order**: Confusing sequence. Test with fresh viewer.
- **Pacing inconsistency**: Random speed changes without reason.
- **No variation**: All shots same length. Boring.
- **Over-sequencing**: Too many cuts/transitions. Exhausting.
- **Under-sequencing**: Too few cuts. Static.
- **Ignoring emotional arc**: No build-up or release. Flat.
- **Breaking continuity**: Screen direction errors, prop mismatches.
- **Revealing too early/late**: Text appears before relevant or after point made.
- **Audio-visual mismatch**: Sound doesn't match picture timing.
- **No transitions where needed**: Abrupt scene changes without indication.
- **Too many transitions**: Distracting. Use cuts as default.

## Advanced Techniques

### 1. Advanced Narrative Structures
- **Hero's journey**: Ordinary world → call to adventure → trials → climax → return.
- **Kishōtenketsu**: Japanese 4-act structure (introduction, development, twist, reconciliation).
- **In medias res**: Start in middle of action, then explain.
- **Framing device**: Story within a story.
- **Unreliable narrator**: Sequence through biased perspective.
- **Reverse chronology**: Tell story backwards.
- **Multiple timelines**: Parallel storylines that converge.

### 2. Advanced Pacing Control
- **Time remapping**: Speed up/slow down clips within sequence.
- **Speed ramps**: Smooth transitions between speeds.
- **Stretch & squeeze**: Extend or compress time for effect.
- **Freeze frames**: Hold on single frame for emphasis.
- **Reverse clips**: Play backwards for surprise or effect.
- **Loop segments**: Repeat short section for emphasis or time fill.

### 3. Complex Intercutting
- **Cross-cutting**: Alternate between two simultaneous actions to build tension.
- **Parallel editing**: Show related but separate storylines.
- **Montage editing**: Condense time with many short shots.
- **Split-screen sequences**: Show multiple perspectives simultaneously.
- **Picture-in-picture**: Show secondary action within main.
- **Multi-camera editing**: Switch between angles of same event.

### 4. Audio-Visual Synchronization
- **Beat-synced editing**: Every cut lands on musical beat.
- **Phoneme sync**: Cuts align with speech mouth movements.
- **Sound bridge**: Audio spans visual transition.
- **L-cut/J-cut**: Audio leads or lags video for smooth flow.
- **Audio-based pacing**: Edit to audio waveform transients.
- **Music video style**: Cuts on every beat or even subdivisions.

### 5. Information Design
- **Progressive disclosure**: Reveal information gradually.
- **Layered explanation**: Simple first, details later.
- **Compare-contrast editing**: Place similar shots side by side.
- **Before-after sequences**: Show transformation.
- **Process documentation**: Step-by-step sequence.
- **Data storytelling**: Sequence data visualizations to build argument.

### 6. Emotional Manipulation
- **Tension building**: Shorten shots, increase music tempo, add SFX.
- **Release**: Lengthen shots, calm music, remove SFX.
- **Surprise**: Subvert expectations with unexpected cut or image.
- **Nostalgia**: Use archival footage, warm color grade, old film effect.
- **Humor**: Timing is everything. Pause before punchline.
- **Sadness**: Slow pacing, soft focus, minor music.
- **Excitement**: Fast cuts, high energy music, dynamic camera.

### 7. Advanced Transitions
- **Invisible transitions**: Match cuts, object blocking, color match.
- **Seamless transitions**: Whip pans, motion blur transitions.
- **Graphic matches**: Cut between similar shapes/colors.
- **Audio transitions**: Sound bridges, crossfades.
- **Thematic transitions**: Cut based on idea rather than time/space.

### 8. Sequence Optimization
- **A/B testing**: Try different sequences, see which performs better.
- **Audience testing**: Watch with target audience, note confusion points.
- **Attention mapping**: Use heatmaps to see where viewers look.
- **Retention analysis**: See where viewers drop off. Adjust sequence.
- **Engagement metrics**: Likes, shares, comments indicate sequence effectiveness.

### 9. Template Sequences
- **Standard explainer**: Hook → problem → solution → benefits → CTA.
- **Tutorial**: Introduction → materials → steps → conclusion.
- **News**: Headline → context → details → implications → next steps.
- **Product demo**: Feature 1 → feature 2 → feature 3 → price → CTA.
- **Vlog**: Intro → main content → outro.
- **Documentary**: Setup → investigation → findings → conclusion.

### 10. Platform-Specific Sequencing
- **TikTok/Reels**: Hook in 0-3s, fast cuts, text on screen, loopable.
- **YouTube**: Longer sequences, chapters, mid-roll considerations.
- **Instagram**: Square/vertical, aesthetic consistency, captions.
- **Twitter/X**: Very short (under 2min), punchy, loopable.
- **LinkedIn**: Professional tone, value-first, subtitles.
- **Facebook**: Auto-play muted → need text/hook.

## Workflow Recommendations

1. **Outline structure**: Plan major sections/acts before editing.
2. **Gather assets**: Collect all footage, audio, graphics needed.
3. **Rough cut**: Place all clips in approximate order. Focus on content, not timing.
4. **Refine sequence**: Reorder clips for best flow. Cut unnecessary material.
5. **Timing**: Adjust clip durations for proper pacing.
6. **Transitions**: Add appropriate transitions between sections.
7. **Audio sequencing**: Add music, SFX, adjust levels over time.
8. **Text/graphics sequencing**: Add titles, lower thirds, callouts in proper order.
9. **Review**: Watch entire sequence. Note awkward moments, pacing issues.
10. **Test with others**: Get feedback on clarity and engagement.
11. **Iterate**: Refine based on feedback.
12. **Final polish**: Color, audio, export.
