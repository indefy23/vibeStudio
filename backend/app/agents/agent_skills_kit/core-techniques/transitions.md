## When to use

Use this skill whenever you need to move from one shot to another. Transitions are fundamental to video editing - they can be invisible (cuts) or highly visible (wipes, dissolves). Understanding when and how to use different transitions is crucial for maintaining narrative flow, creating visual interest, and conveying meaning.

## Core Principles

### 1. The Cut - Your Primary Tool
- **Definition**: An immediate change from one shot to another. The most common and often invisible transition.
- **When to use**: 90% of your transitions should be cuts. They're fast, unobtrusive, and maintain continuity.
- **Cutting on action**: Cut while subject is moving. The motion masks the cut, making it invisible.
- **Cutting on dialogue**: Cut between speakers during conversation. Use eyeline matches to maintain spatial continuity.
- **Cutting on beat**: Align cuts to musical beats for rhythmic satisfaction.
- **Jump cut**: Cut between two similar shots of same subject from similar angle. Creates disorientation or shows time passing. Use intentionally.

### 2. Standard Transitions
- **Fade In/Out**: Gradual transition to/from black (or white).
  - **Fade In**: Beginning of scene, video starts from black.
  - **Fade Out**: End of scene, video fades to black.
  - **Duration**: 0.5-2 seconds typically. Longer fades feel more dramatic/meditative.
  - **Meaning**: Fades often indicate passage of time, scene beginning/end, or emotional transition.

- **Dissolve**: One shot gradually replaces another by blending together.
  - **Duration**: 0.5-1.5 seconds typical.
  - **Meaning**: Passage of time, connection between scenes, dreamy/emotional effect, location change.
  - **Overuse warning**: Dissolves can look dated if overused. Use purposefully.

- **Wipe**: One shot pushes another off screen in a pattern.
  - **Types**: Edge wipe, center wipe, clock wipe, shape wipe, custom matte wipe.
  - **Duration**: 0.3-1 second.
  - **Meaning**: Can indicate location change, time passage, or be stylistic (Star Wars).
  - **Modern use**: Rare in serious film, common in corporate videos, presentations, some music videos.

### 3. Creative Transitions
- **Match Cut**: Cut between two shots with similar composition, shape, or action.
  - **Visual match**: Similar framing, color, or shape (e.g., round moon to round face).
  - **Action match**: Character begins action in one shot, completes it in next (e.g., throwing ball).
  - **Meaning**: Creates thematic connection, shows parallel action, smooth time/space jump.

- **Smash Cut**: Abrupt, jarring cut from quiet/scene to loud/violent or vice versa.
  - **Purpose**: Shock, humor, contrast. Often used for comedic effect or to show sudden change.
  - **Example**: Peaceful scene suddenly cuts to chaos.

- **Cross Dissolve with Motion**: Dissolve while simultaneously zooming, panning, or rotating one or both shots.
  - **Effect**: More dynamic than static dissolve.
  - **Use**: Music videos, dynamic montages, energetic content.

- **Glitch Transition**: Digital distortion, RGB shift, pixelation, or data moshing during transition.
  - **Duration**: Very short (0.1-0.3s).
  - **Style**: Tech, hacker, cyberpunk, high-energy content.
  - **Implementation**: Use glitch overlays or built-in glitch transitions.

- **Whip Pan**: Extremely fast pan motion that blurs the image, often used as transition.
  - **Direction**: Can pan left-to-right or right-to-left.
  - **Speed**: Very fast (0.1-0.3s). Creates blur streak.
  - **Use**: Action sequences, fast-paced edits, energetic transitions.
  - **Variation**: Zoom whip (fast zoom instead of pan).

- **Spin Blur / Barrel Roll**: Rotate and blur one shot into the next.
  - **Effect**: Disorienting, energetic.
  - **Use**: Sports, action, music videos.

- **Mask Transition**: Use a shape (circle, square, custom) to reveal next shot.
  - **Animation**: Shape grows or moves to uncover next shot.
  - **Use**: Product reveals, logo reveals, creative storytelling.

- **Object Transition**: Use an element in the shot to cover the screen, then reveal next shot from behind it.
  - **Example**: Person walks past camera, screen goes dark, then reveals new scene.
  - **Use**: Seamless, narrative transitions.

### 4. Motion Graphics Transitions
- **Shape Layer Wipe**: Animated shape (rectangle, circle) expands to fill screen, then contracts to reveal next shot.
- **Text Transition**: Text animates in, covers screen, then animates out revealing next shot.
- **Particle Transition**: Particles (dust, sparks, smoke) fill screen, then clear to reveal next shot.
- **Liquid Transition**: Fluid simulation or liquid shape morphs from one shot to another.
- **3D Flip / Card Flip**: Shot appears to flip like a card to reveal next shot.
- **Zoom Blur Transition**: Rapid zoom into one shot, then zoom out of next shot.
- **Parallax Transition**: Layers move at different speeds to create depth during transition.

### 5. Audio Transitions
- **J-cut**: Audio from next scene starts before video changes.
  - **Purpose**: Smooth forward momentum, anticipation.
  - **Duration**: 0.5-2 seconds lead.

- **L-cut**: Video changes first, audio from previous scene continues.
  - **Purpose**: Emotional carryover, continuity, reaction shots.
  - **Duration**: 0.5-3 seconds linger.

- **Crossfade**: Audio from two scenes overlap and crossfade.
  - **Purpose**: Smooth audio transition, blend music beds.
  - **Duration**: 1-3 seconds.

- **Sound bridge**: Use a sound effect that spans the transition (e.g., door slam continues into next scene).

### 6. Transition Selection Guidelines
- **Match transition to content**:
  - **Fast-paced content**: Quick cuts, whip pans, glitch transitions (0.1-0.3s).
  - **Emotional/dramatic**: Dissolves, fades, slow match cuts (1-2s).
  - **Corporate/presentations**: Simple cuts, basic wipes, fades.
  - **Music videos**: Creative transitions synced to beat.
  - **Documentary**: Mostly cuts, occasional dissolves for time passage.

- **Match transition to genre**:
  - **Action**: Cuts, whip pans, smash cuts.
  - **Comedy**: Smash cuts, match cuts, creative transitions for jokes.
  - **Drama**: Cuts, dissolves, long fades.
  - **Sci-fi/tech**: Glitch transitions, digital wipes, 3D flips.

- **Don't overuse**: Any transition other than cut should be used sparingly (5-10% of total transitions).
- **Consistency**: Establish a transition style and stick to it within a video. Random styles look unprofessional.
- **Purpose**: Every transition should have a reason - narrative, rhythmic, or thematic.

## Technical Guidelines

### Transition Duration
- **Cut**: Instant (0 frames).
- **Fast transition**: 0.1-0.3 seconds (whip pan, glitch, fast wipe).
- **Standard transition**: 0.5-1 second (dissolve, slow wipe, most motion graphics).
- **Slow transition**: 1-2 seconds (fade, slow dissolve, dramatic reveal).
- **Very slow**: 2+ seconds (artistic, meditative).

### Implementation
- **Edit points**: Mark in/out points for each clip with handles (extra frames before/after for transitions).
- **Handles**: Need at least 10-15 frames (0.3-0.5s at 30fps) before and after for most transitions.
- **Layering**: Some transitions require overlapping clips on timeline (dissolve, crossfade).
- **Motion graphics transitions**: Often require pre-rendered elements or complex compositions.
- **Audio during transitions**: Ensure audio continues smoothly. Use J-cuts/L-cuts or audio crossfades.

### Software-Specific
- **Premiere Pro**: Effects panel → Video Transitions. Drag onto cut point.
- **DaVinci Resolve**: Effects library → Transitions. Drag to edit point.
- **After Effects**: Use presets or create custom with shape layers, masks, effects.
- **Remotion**: Use `Transition` components or custom animations with `interpolate`.

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

- **Overusing fancy transitions**: Every transition being a wipe/glitch looks amateurish. Use cuts as default.
- **Inconsistent transition duration**: Random durations feel unpolished. Establish a standard.
- **No handles**: Trying to apply transition without enough footage results in black frames or stuttering.
- **Audio popping**: Audio not crossfaded during video transitions causes pops. Always handle audio.
- **Breaking continuity**: Transitions that ignore spatial or temporal continuity confuse viewers.
- **Too slow for content**: Long fades/dissolves in fast-paced videos kill energy.
- **Too fast for content**: Quick transitions in slow, dramatic scenes feel rushed.
- **Transition for transition's sake**: Using transitions just because you can. Every transition should serve a purpose.
- **Ignoring audio**: Visual transition but audio cuts abruptly. Use audio bridges.
- **Poor timing**: Transition ending before or after intended moment. Fine-tune.

## Advanced Techniques

### 1. Invisible Transitions
- **Match cut**: Seamless cut based on visual similarity. Feels like one continuous shot.
- **Object blocking**: Subject passes in front of camera, cut while obscured, reveal new shot.
- **Whip pan as cut**: Very fast whip pan (1 frame) can feel like a cut but with energy.
- **Color match**: Cut between shots with identical color palette and brightness.
- **Motion continuation**: Subject moves in same direction across cut.

### 2. Layered Transitions
- **Dual-layer transition**: Two different transition types overlapping (e.g., dissolve + zoom).
- **Text overlay transition**: Text animates across screen, revealing next shot behind it.
- **Graphic element transition**: Animated logo, shape, or icon transitions between shots.
- **Particle transition**: Particles (dust, sparks) fill screen, then clear to reveal next shot.

### 3. Rhythm-Based Transitions
- **Beat-synced transitions**: Every transition lands exactly on a musical beat.
- **Polyrhythmic transitions**: Some transitions on beat, some off-beat for complexity.
- **Accelerando transitions**: Transition duration shortens as sequence progresses (builds energy).
- **Ritardando transitions**: Transition duration lengthens (creates release).

### 4. Narrative Transitions
- **Flashback transition**: Fade to white/black, then fade in to past scene.
- **Dream sequence**: Dissolve with blur, soft focus, desaturation.
- **Time passage**: Dissolve with clock hands spinning, calendar pages flipping.
- **Location change**: Wipe with map, zoom out/in from Earth, door opening/closing.
- **Perspective shift**: 180° spin, POV change transition.

### 5. Technical Advanced
- **Custom matte transitions**: Use alpha channel from image/video to shape transition.
- **3D camera transitions**: Animate 3D camera through layers to transition.
- **Displacement map transitions**: Use grayscale map to distort one shot into another.
- **Time remapping during transition**: Speed up/slow down during transition for dynamic effect.
- **Multi-layer dissolves**: Dissolve through intermediate images (e.g., A → B → C).

### 6. Transition Design Principles
- **Directionality**: Transitions should have a direction (left-to-right, zoom-in, etc.) that guides the eye.
- **Continuity of motion**: If subject moves right in first shot, transition should continue that motion.
- **Color continuity**: Transition colors should complement or contrast intentionally.
- **Scale continuity**: Maintain similar scale or have purposeful scale change.
- **Energy matching**: Transition energy should match scene energy (fast transition for fast scene).

## Workflow Recommendations

1. **Edit with cuts first**: Build your entire edit using only cuts. Get story and pacing right.
2. **Identify transition points**: Mark where you might want special transitions (emotional moments, scene changes, beat drops).
3. **Choose transition type**: Select appropriate transition for each marked point based on content and emotion.
4. **Add transitions**: Apply transitions. Start with simple ones (dissolve, fade) before trying complex.
5. **Fine-tune timing**: Adjust transition duration to feel natural. Often shorter than you think.
6. **Audio check**: Ensure audio transitions are smooth. Add J-cuts/L-cuts if needed.
7. **Review in context**: Watch entire sequence. Transitions should feel cohesive, not random.
8. **Simplify**: If a transition feels distracting, replace with cut.
9. **Test on different screens**: Transitions may look different on phone vs. computer.
10. **Render test**: Export a segment to verify transitions look as intended.
