## When to use

Use this skill whenever you need to edit a TikTok news video with a tense, high-energy, hype-driven rhythm. This style is designed to capture attention immediately and maintain high retention through fast pacing, dynamic transitions, and beat-synced editing.

## Core Principles

### 1. Tense Rhythm Through Pacing
- **Quick cuts are essential**: Average shot length should be 0.5-1.5 seconds. Never let a shot linger beyond 2 seconds unless it's a deliberate dramatic pause.
- **Build tension through acceleration**: Start with slightly longer shots in the hook (1-2s), then progressively shorten as the video continues.
- **Use the beat as your metronome**: Every cut, transition, and effect should land on a musical beat. This creates subconscious satisfaction and drives the rhythm.

### 2. Hype Energy Through Visual Dynamics
- **Transitions should be snappy, not smooth**: Whip pans, zoom transitions, and glitch effects with durations of 0.1-0.3 seconds create energy. Avoid smooth fades or dissolves.
- **Strategic speed ramping**: Use speed ramps (0.3x, 0.5x, 1.5x, 2x) to create emphasis and variation. Slow motion on key moments, speed-ups on transitions.
- **Overlay effects sparingly but impactfully**: Film burns, light leaks, grain, and digital glitches add texture and intensity. Use them on beat drops or dramatic moments.
- **Text animations must be punchy**: Pop-in, typewriter, or glitch text effects. Text should appear and disappear quickly, never lingering unnecessarily.

### 3. News Urgency Through Structure
- **Hook in 0-3 seconds**: The first frame must grab attention. Use a shocking statement, breaking news banner, or visual hook.
- **Layered information**: Combine spoken word, on-screen text, and graphics to deliver news quickly. Redundancy is good - viewers may miss one element but catch another.
- **Breaking news aesthetic**: Use news-style graphics (lower thirds, banners, countdown timers) but with a modern, high-energy twist.

### 4. Audio as the Foundation
- **Music drives everything**: Choose high-BPM (140-180) trap, phonk, or electronic tracks with clear, punchy beats.
- **Sound effects are punctuation**: Whoosh, impact, swoosh, glitch, alarm sounds should accent cuts and transitions. They should be audible but not overpowering.
- **Voiceover urgency**: If using narration, deliver lines with energy and urgency. Short sentences. Pauses for effect.
- **Audio sync is non-negotiable**: Cuts must land on beats. Effects must hit on transients. This is what makes the rhythm feel "tight."

## Technical Guidelines

### Format & Quality
- **Resolution**: 1080x1920 (9:16 vertical) minimum. 720p acceptable but 1080p preferred.
- **Frame rate**: 30fps or 60fps. Higher frame rates allow for smoother slow-motion.
- **Codec**: H.264 or H.265 with high bitrate (5-10 Mbps) for quality.
- **Full screen portrait**: Ensure important elements are centered and not obscured by UI elements.

### Editing Workflow
1. **Start with audio**: Import your music track first. Mark all beat points on your timeline. This becomes your editing skeleton.
2. **Assembly edit**: Place all footage in sequence, ignoring length initially. Get the story/flow right.
3. **Cut to beat**: Trim every clip so cuts happen exactly on beats. This is the most important step for rhythm.
4. **Add transitions**: Insert whip pans, zoom transitions, or glitch effects between shots. Keep them short (0.1-0.3s).
5. **Speed ramps**: Identify key moments and add speed ramps. Slow down for emphasis, speed up for energy.
6. **Text & graphics**: Add breaking news banners, lower thirds, and animated text. Sync their appearance to beats.
7. **Sound design**: Layer in sound effects on transitions and key moments. Duck music during speech.
8. **Color grade**: Apply high-contrast, saturated look. Slight teal & orange split toning works well.
9. **Overlay effects**: Add film burns, light leaks, or grain sparingly on dramatic moments.
10. **Final mix**: Balance all audio elements. Music should be present but not overpowering.

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

- **Smooth transitions**: They kill energy. Use snappy, sharp transitions.
- **Long shots**: Anything over 2 seconds breaks the rhythm. Trim aggressively.
- **Off-beat editing**: If cuts aren't on the beat, the video feels sloppy. Lock to the music.
- **Low contrast/bright colors**: The style needs punch. Increase contrast and saturation.
- **No text overlays**: TikTok viewers expect text. It aids comprehension and retention.
- **Horizontal footage**: Must be vertical. Crop or reframe accordingly.
- **Overusing effects**: Too many overlays become distracting. Use effects purposefully.
- **Weak hook**: If the first 3 seconds don't grab, viewers scroll. Front-load your strongest element.

## Advanced Techniques

### Beat Drops & Build-Ups
- **Pre-drop tension**: In the 4-8 beats before a drop, shorten shot lengths even more (0.3-0.8s) and add rising sound effects.
- **Drop impact**: On the beat drop, use a combination of: a whip pan transition, a "whoosh" sound effect, a text pop-in, and possibly a brief screen shake.
- **Post-drop release**: After the drop, you can slightly lengthen shots (1-2s) to give viewers a moment to process before building again.

### Layered Storytelling
- **Triple delivery**: For important points, use: (1) spoken word, (2) on-screen text, (3) supporting visual. This ensures the message lands.
- **Visual metaphors**: Use quick cuts to related imagery that amplifies the news point. For example, if talking about "explosive growth," cut to an explosion or fast-motion graph.
- **Reaction shots**: Include brief (0.5s) reaction shots of people's faces or audience responses to create emotional connection.

### Text Hierarchy
- **Headline text**: Large, bold, animated. Used for main points. Appears 1-2 seconds.
- **Subtitle/caption text**: Smaller, for clarification. Can stay on screen longer (2-4s).
- **Emphasis text**: Single words or short phrases that pop in sync with sound effects.

## When NOT to Use This Style

- **Serious/solemn news**: This style is for engaging, attention-grabbing content. Not appropriate for sensitive or tragic news.
- **Complex explanations**: The fast pace doesn't allow for deep dives. Use for headlines, summaries, or teasers.
- **Long-form content**: This is designed for 15-60 second videos. Beyond that, the pace becomes exhausting.
- **Audiences expecting traditional news**: Older demographics may find this style jarring or unprofessional.
