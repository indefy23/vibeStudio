## When to use

Use this skill when creating cinematic-style videos that aim to replicate the look, feel, and emotional impact of professional films. This style emphasizes visual storytelling through deliberate pacing, careful shot composition, and sophisticated color grading.

## Core Principles

### 1. Deliberate Pacing
- **Embrace longer takes**: Cinematic editing often uses longer shot durations (3-10 seconds) to allow viewers to absorb the scene and build emotional connection.
- **Vary rhythm intentionally**: Mix slow, contemplative moments with faster cuts to create dynamic emotional arcs. Don't maintain constant pace.
- **Cut on performance**: In dialogue scenes, cut based on actor's beats and emotional peaks, not on a predetermined timer.
- **Use pauses strategically**: Silence and stillness are powerful cinematic tools. Let moments breathe.

### 2. Visual Composition & Shot Selection
- **Establish geography**: Use establishing shots and master shots to orient viewers in space. Maintain screen direction consistency.
- **Vary shot sizes strategically**:
  - Extreme long shots (ELS) for scale and context
  - Long shots (LS) for full-body action
  - Medium shots (MS) for interaction
  - Close-ups (CU) for emotion and detail
  - Extreme close-ups (ECU) for intensity
- **Follow the 180-degree rule**: Maintain consistent screen direction for spatial continuity.
- **Use motivated camera movement**: Pan, tilt, dolly, and handheld should serve the story, not decorate it.

### 3. Color Grading for Mood
- **Start with color correction**: Fix exposure, white balance, and match shots before grading.
- **Develop a cohesive palette**: Choose a color scheme that supports the narrative (e.g., teal & orange for action, desaturated for drama, warm tones for romance).
- **Use color to guide attention**: Slightly desaturate background elements, keep subject vibrant.
- **Maintain consistency**: Color grade in scenes, not individual shots. Ensure smooth transitions between differently graded shots.
- **Consider film emulation**: Add subtle grain, slight highlight rolloff, and appropriate contrast to mimic film stock characteristics.

### 4. Sound Design Integration
- **Layered audio**: Combine dialogue, ambient sound, foley, and music to create immersive soundscape.
- **Silence as a tool**: Use moments of silence for dramatic effect.
- **Audio bridges**: Use sound from the next scene to smooth transitions (L-cut and J-cut).
- **Room tone**: Always include ambient room tone in dialogue scenes to avoid dead air.

### 5. Narrative Flow
- **Match cuts for thematic connection**: Cut between shots with similar shapes, compositions, or actions to create visual metaphors.
- **Cross-cutting for parallel action**: Build tension by alternating between simultaneous events.
- **Montage sequences**: Use rapid cuts to compress time or show progression (training montage, passage of time).
- **Eyeline matches**: When a character looks off-screen, show what they're looking at to maintain continuity.

## Technical Guidelines

### Format & Resolution
- **Resolution**: 4K (3840x2160) or higher for maximum flexibility in post
- **Frame rate**: 24fps or 25fps for authentic film look. 30fps acceptable for digital cinema.
- **Codec**: Shoot in high-bitrate codec (ProRes, DNxHD, or high-bitrate H.264/H.265)
- **Aspect ratio**: 16:9 standard, but consider 2.35:1 or 2.39:1 anamorphic for feature film look

### Color Space & Dynamic Range
- **Log profile**: Shoot in S-Log, C-Log, or similar log profiles to preserve dynamic range
- **Color space**: Rec.709 for delivery, but work in wide gamut (Rec.2020/P3) if possible
- **Bit depth**: 10-bit or higher for smooth color gradations

### Aspect Ratios & Letterboxing
- **Cinematic aspect ratios**: 2.35:1, 2.39:1, or 1.85:1 for different film looks
- **Letterboxing**: Add black bars in post, not in camera, for flexibility
- **Safe areas**: Keep important action within title-safe and action-safe zones

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

- **Jump cuts**: Unless intentional, maintain continuity. Use cutaways or B-roll to hide jump cuts.
- **Inconsistent color**: Shots that don't match in color are jarring. Use color matching tools.
- **Over-cutting**: Too many cuts make a scene feel rushed and disjointed. Trust your actors and let scenes play.
- **Poor audio**: Bad audio ruins cinematic feel. Invest in good sound recording and mixing.
- **Ignoring screen direction**: Breaking the 180-degree rule confuses viewers.
- **Heavy-handed effects**: Overuse of filters, transitions, or effects breaks immersion.
- **Unmotivated camera movement**: Every movement should have a narrative purpose.

## Advanced Techniques

### 1. Invisible Editing
- **Cut on motion**: Cuts are less noticeable when they occur during movement.
- **Match frames**: Cut to a shot with similar composition or color to create seamless transitions.
- **Audio continuity**: Use consistent ambient sound across cuts to smooth visual transitions.

### 2. Emotional Pacing
- **Hold on reaction shots**: Let actors' performances land. A 2-second reaction can be more powerful than dialogue.
- **Slow motion for emphasis**: Use selectively (5-10% of video) to highlight key moments.
- **Speed ramping**: Smoothly transition between normal and slow/fast speeds within a single shot.

### 3. Visual Storytelling
- **Show, don't tell**: Use visuals to convey information instead of dialogue or text.
- **Symbolic imagery**: Incorporate visual metaphors that reinforce themes.
- **Foreshadowing**: Include subtle visual hints early that pay off later.

### 4. Color Psychology
- **Warm colors** (reds, oranges, yellows): passion, warmth, danger, energy
- **Cool colors** (blues, cyans): calm, sadness, detachment, technology
- **High contrast**: tension, drama, conflict
- **Low contrast**: nostalgia, softness, romance
- **Desaturated**: bleakness, seriousness, period pieces

## Workflow Recommendations

1. **Assembly edit**: Place all shots in sequence, get story right
2. **Rough cut**: Trim to proper timing, establish pacing
3. **Fine cut**: Refine cuts, add transitions, polish rhythm
4. **Color correction**: Match shots, fix exposure, white balance
5. **Color grading**: Apply stylistic look, maintain consistency
6. **Sound design**: Add effects, mix audio, balance levels
7. **Final mix**: Master audio, final color tweaks, quality check
