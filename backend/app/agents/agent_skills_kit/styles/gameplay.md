## When to use

Use this skill when editing gaming videos that showcase gameplay footage with high energy and engagement. This style is perfect for highlight reels, kill montages, funny moments, esports clips, and tutorial content where the focus is on the game itself rather than the player's face.

## Core Principles

### 1. Pacing & Rhythm
- **Fast cuts for action**: During intense gameplay moments, use quick cuts (0.3-1 second) between different angles or kills to maintain adrenaline.
- **Slow-motion for impact**: Highlight clutch plays, headshots, or epic moments with 50-80% slow motion. Use sparingly (5-10% of video) for maximum effect.
- **Speed ramps**: Smoothly transition between normal and slow/fast speeds within a single clip to emphasize key actions.
- **Hold on reaction**: After a big play, hold the reaction shot (player or teammate) for 1-2 seconds to let the moment sink in.

### 2. Visual Enhancement
- **Zoom & pan**: Use dynamic zoom-ins on important details (enemy location, loot, UI elements). Smooth zoom transitions work better than static cuts.
- **Highlight effects**: Add subtle glow, outline, or color pop on the player's crosshair, health bar, or important game elements.
- **Screen shake**: On explosions, kills, or impacts, add slight camera shake effect (5-10% intensity) to convey force.
- **Hit markers & damage numbers**: Make damage numbers and hit markers more visible with animation or color.

### 3. Audio Design
- **Game audio balance**: Keep in-game audio (footsteps, gunshots, abilities) prominent. This is crucial for immersion.
- **Commentary integration**: If adding voiceover, duck game audio during speech. Use EQ to make voice cut through.
- **Sound effects**: Add cinematic whooshes, impacts, and swooshes on cuts and kills. Use sparingly to enhance, not overpower.
- **Music selection**: Choose high-energy tracks that match the game's mood (electronic for shooters, epic orchestral for RPGs, lo-fi for casual). Sync cuts to music beats.

### 4. Text & Graphics
- **Kill counters**: Display kill/death/assist stats in an attractive, non-intrusive way.
- **Player names & roles**: Use lower thirds to identify players, especially in team games.
- **Commentary text**: Reinforce funny or insightful commentary with on-screen text that appears with animation.
- **Memes & reactions**: Include meme images or GIFs that pop in sync with audio cues or funny moments.
- **Progress indicators**: Show round number, match time, or objective status when relevant.

### 5. Structure & Flow
- **Hook immediately**: Start with your best clip within first 3 seconds. No long intros.
- **Build narrative**: Even highlight reels benefit from a loose story: setup → conflict → climax → resolution.
- **Vary clip types**: Mix clutch plays, funny moments, and impressive skills to create emotional variety.
- **End strong**: Finish with your second-best clip after the best one at the start. Leave viewers wanting more.

## Technical Guidelines

### Resolution & Frame Rate
- **Capture resolution**: 1080p or 1440p at minimum. 4K if your PC can handle it.
- **Frame rate**: Match your game's FPS (usually 60, 120, or 144). Higher frame rates allow for smoother slow-motion.
- **Export**: 1080p 60fps is standard for YouTube gaming. Use high bitrate (20-40 Mbps) for quality.

### Aspect Ratio
- **Standard**: 16:9 for most platforms (YouTube, Twitch highlights).
- **Vertical**: 9:16 for TikTok/Reels/Shorts. Crop center or use dynamic reframing.
- **Ultrawide**: 21:9 if capturing ultrawide gameplay, but be aware most viewers watch on 16:9 screens.

### Color & Effects
- **Game color preservation**: Don't oversaturate or change game's intended color palette. Minor contrast boost is okay.
- **Night vision/thermal**: If game has these modes, ensure they're clearly distinguishable from normal vision.
- **UI visibility**: Keep important HUD elements visible. Don't obscure critical information with graphics.

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

- **Long boring segments**: Cut any downtime, loading screens, or repetitive grinding. Keep only the action.
- **Poor audio balance**: Game audio too quiet or voiceover too loud. Use proper mixing.
- **Over-editing**: Too many effects, zooms, and cuts can be distracting. Use effects purposefully.
- **Copyright music**: Using copyrighted music will get videos muted or demonetized. Use royalty-free or YouTube Audio Library.
- **Spoilers without warning**: If covering story games, warn viewers or use timestamps.
- **Inconsistent volume**: Normalize audio levels across all clips. No sudden loud/quiet jumps.
- **Bad thumbnails**: Invest time in good thumbnails. They're as important as the video itself.
- **Ignoring community guidelines**: Some games have specific content policies. Know them before posting.

## Advanced Techniques

### 1. Multi-Camera Editing
- **Gameplay + facecam**: If you have facecam footage, use picture-in-picture or side-by-side during key moments.
- **Multiple angles**: Switch between different in-game camera angles (first-person, third-person, killcam) for variety.
- **Reaction shots**: Include your face reaction (if available) after clutch plays or funny moments.

### 2. Narrative Highlight Reels
- **Thematic editing**: Group clips by theme (best snipes, clutch 1v5s, funny fails) rather than chronological.
- **Music video style**: Edit entire montage to music beat, with every cut landing on a beat. Use color grading to match music mood.
- **Story arcs**: Create mini-stories within the montage: struggle → comeback → victory.

### 3. Educational Gameplay
- **Tutorial segments**: Use zoom, circles, and arrows to highlight specific techniques.
- **Before/after comparisons**: Split screen showing wrong vs right approach.
- **Slow-motion breakdown**: 50% speed with commentary explaining decision-making.

### 4. Esports Production
- **Player focus**: Follow specific player's POV throughout a round. Use their name graphic.
- **Strategy visualization**: Draw arrows or circles to show positioning and rotations.
- **Stat overlays**: Display K/D, economy, timer, and other relevant esports data.

## Workflow Recommendations

1. **Capture**: Record gameplay at high quality. Also record facecam/commentary if needed.
2. **Organize**: Tag and categorize clips during or after recording (kills, funny, clutch, fail).
3. **Select best clips**: Choose only the highest impact moments. Be ruthless in cutting.
4. **Music selection**: Pick track that matches energy level. Download or use royalty-free.
5. **Assembly edit**: Place clips in sequence synced to music beats.
6. **Effects & graphics**: Add zooms, text, highlights, sound effects.
7. **Audio mix**: Balance game audio, music, and voiceover. Use compression and limiting.
8. **Color grade**: Minor adjustments for consistency. Don't overdo.
9. **Review**: Watch through. Cut anything boring. Adjust pacing.
10. **Thumbnail & upload**: Create compelling thumbnail, write description with timestamps.
